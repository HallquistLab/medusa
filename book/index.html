MEDuSA

From BOLD time series to trial-level inference

Published

November 18, 2026

Introduction

Multilevel Event-Related Deconvolved Signal Analysis (medusa) estimates continuous event-informed latent activity from fMRI BOLD time series, aligns and de-mixes overlapping trial epochs, propagates observation-noise covariance into parcel panels, and fits calibrated multilevel, trajectory, and measurement-error-corrected connectivity models across participants.

Imagine a reinforcement-learning task in which participants decide when to respond and then receive feedback. Choice deliberation varies in duration from trial to trial, while feedback triggers a phasic prediction error. Because hemodynamic responses span 15–30 seconds, BOLD signals evoked by cues, variable-duration decisions, and feedback overlap substantially even when the underlying neural processes are distinct. Depending on the scientific question, an investigator may want to test:

  • whether a trial-by-trial prediction error modulates feedback-onset activity within a prespecified parcel,
  • whether neural activity during deliberation scales in firing rate (height) or total integrated activity across reaction times,
  • how a population effect evolves continuously across an event-locked lag trajectory, or
  • whether trial-to-trial coupling between two regions changes with task context (psychophysiological interaction, PPI) beyond shared physiological noise.

Michael Hallquist’s 2022 MEDuSA Quant Talk introduced the clock-task motivation for placing deconvolution, event alignment, and multilevel modeling into a unified sequence. This book documents how the standalone medusa R package implements that framework end to end—walking through the statistical logic, modeling choices, and practical diagnostics at each stage.

Why use MEDuSA—and how does the pipeline fit together?

Common fMRI analysis strategies each address part of the problem:

  • Condition-level task GLMs test prespecified canonical BOLD predictors efficiently, while collapsing trial-to-trial variability unless parametric modulators are specified for one model at a time.
  • Finite impulse response (FIR) models relax HRF shape assumptions for condition averages, while becoming difficult to estimate under rapid jittered trial-by-trial modulation and variable reaction-time intervals.
  • Single-trial GLMs (LSS / LSA) estimate one scalar amplitude per event under an assumed HRF, rather than a continuous event-locked trajectory across lags with cross-fitted vascular timing.
  • Blind deconvolution (deconvolve_nlreg(), deconvolve_reglin()) reconstructs continuous time series without event timestamps, leaving neurovascular delay, task-free noise scaling, and trial-level error covariances unanchored.

For a side-by-side simulation comparing Parametric GLM, Condition-Modulated FIR, LSS beta-series, and MEDuSA across single-phase, rapid multi-phase RL, and biphasic neural regimes, see Conceptual foundations §5.

MEDuSA combines out-of-sample event-informed neurovascular identification with continuous regularized linear deconvolution and operator-propagated trial inference. Because every linear transformation from raw BOLD to trial readouts is retained explicitly, downstream epoch regressions, parcel aggregations, multilevel models, and connectivity estimators carry both the resolution matrix (how neighboring events and lags mix in expectation) and the propagated error covariance (how observation noise varies and correlates across trials, lags, and parcels).

How to read this book

  1. Begin with Conceptual foundations for the unifying measurement-and-structural framework (\(y \to \widehat{a} \to \widehat{z}\), including the end-to-end data-flow schematic and notation and S3 object map) and A worked analysis for an executable 12-participant walkthrough from simulated BOLD matrices to group inference.
  2. Read the chapters in The MEDuSA pipeline (Steps 0–6), accompanied by the Visual QC and Troubleshooting Gallery. Each stepwise chapter opens with a thumbnail Overview and clean Goals, Inputs, and Outputs tables, walks step by step through each Substep and Choice Point from simple intuition to the full estimator, summarizes Recommended Settings, and closes with Practical Considerations, Diagnostics, and Scope.
  3. Explore Spatial Graph Pooling: Voxelwise and Parcel-Wise NIfTI Workflows for an MNI152 cortical-ribbon walkthrough comparing geodesic bilateral graph pooling, SNR-stratified deconvolution, voxelwise Graph-EB mapping (write_nifti_ts()), and parcel-wise panel backprojection (backproject_medusa()) visualized with ggbrain.
  4. Consult Methodological deep dives for question-driven chapters examining fast-TR REML AR(\(p\)) prewhitening vs. lag tapering, propagating BOLD noise into trial standard errors, simultaneous trajectory bands and familywise calibration, latent connectivity beyond per-trial disattenuation, and what validation proves—plus building study-specific calibration checks.
