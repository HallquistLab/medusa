MEDuSA

From BOLD time series to trial-level inference

Published

November 13, 2026

Introduction

Multilevel Event-Related Deconvolved Signal Analysis (medusa) estimates continuous event-informed latent activity from fMRI BOLD time series, aligns and de-mixes overlapping trial epochs, propagates observation-noise covariance into parcel panels, and fits calibrated multilevel, trajectory, and measurement-error-corrected connectivity models across participants.

Imagine a reinforcement-learning task in which participants decide when to respond and then receive feedback. Choice deliberation varies in duration from trial to trial, while feedback triggers a phasic prediction error. Because hemodynamic responses span 15–30 seconds, BOLD signals evoked by cues, variable-duration decisions, and feedback overlap heavily even when the underlying neural processes are distinct. Depending on the scientific hypothesis, an investigator may want to test:

  • whether a trial-by-trial prediction error modulates feedback-onset activity within a prespecified parcel,
  • whether neural activity during deliberation scales in firing rate (height) or total integrated mass across reaction times,
  • how a population effect evolves continuously across an event-locked lag trajectory, or
  • whether trial-to-trial coupling between two regions changes with task context (psychophysiological interaction, PPI) beyond shared physiological noise.

Michael Hallquist’s 2022 MEDuSA Quant Talk introduced the clock-task motivation for placing deconvolution, event alignment, and multilevel modeling into a unified sequence, while highlighting open methodological challenges around amplitude scaling, temporal resolution, uncertainty propagation, and regional connectivity. This book documents how the standalone medusa R package solves each stage end to end, what assumptions each step makes, and where fundamental identifiability limits remain.

Why use MEDuSA—and how does the six-step pipeline fit together?

Standard fMRI analysis tools each address part of the problem:

  • Condition-level task GLMs test prespecified canonical BOLD predictors efficiently, but collapse trial-to-trial variability unless parametric modulators are specified upfront for one model at a time.
  • Finite impulse response (FIR) models relax HRF shape assumptions for condition averages, but struggle with rapid jittered trial-by-trial modulation and variable reaction-time intervals.
  • Single-trial GLMs (LSS / LSA) estimate one scalar amplitude per event under an assumed HRF, but do not directly yield a continuous event-locked trajectory across lags or separate cross-fitted vascular timing from continuous background dynamics.
  • Blind deconvolution (deconvolve_nlreg(), deconvolve_reglin()) reconstructs continuous time series without event timestamps, but suffers from HRF-delay ambiguity, task-contaminated variance scaling, oversmoothing, and lack of an operator-propagated trial error covariance.

MEDuSA combines out-of-sample event-informed neurovascular identification with continuous regularized linear deconvolution and operator-propagated trial inference. Because every linear transformation from raw BOLD to trial readouts is retained explicitly, downstream epoch regressions, parcel aggregations, multilevel models, and connectivity estimators know both the resolution matrix (how neighboring events and lags mix in the mean) and the propagated noise covariance (how observation noise correlates across trials, lags, and parcels).

Pipeline step Primary medusa function(s) Core question answered
1. Estimate Cross-Run HRF & Noise hrf_library(), estimate_crossrun_hrf() What HRF shape and AR(\(p\)) noise covariance govern each run and parcel without circularly fitting the target run’s own task amplitudes?
2. Deconvolve Continuous Signal deconvolve_crossrun_hrf(), diagnose_crossrun_recovery() What continuous latent time course is supported on the acquisition grid when event-informed GLS regression anchors noise scaling, adaptive glitch weighting, and GCV smoothing?
3. Align Epochs & De-Mix Overlap decon_epoch_regression() How do we read out event-locked lags, subtract non-target bleed, unmix adjacent target trials, model variable durations, and propagate per-trial standard errors?
4. Assemble Parcel Panels decon_epoch_regression(..., parcel_groups = ...) How should voxels be precision-weighted into parcels while propagating spatial noise correlations, cohort weights, and ZCA-whitened predictors?
5. Fit Group Multilevel & Trajectory Models mixed_by(), medusa_multilag_infer() How do we test planned lag slopes, full multi-lag trajectories, simultaneous bands, and window contrasts across participants?
6. Estimate Connectivity & PPI decon_connectivity_repair(), medusa_fit_mmom() How do we disattenuate noisy neural predictors on the right-hand side while accounting for shared regional measurement error?

How to read this book

  1. Begin with Conceptual foundations for the unifying linear-operator framework (\(y \to \hat{s} \to \hat{z}\)) and A worked analysis for an executable 12-participant walkthrough from simulated BOLD matrices to group inference.
  2. Read the six step chapters in The six steps. Every step chapter opens with a plain-language Overview, explicit Goals, structured Inputs and Outputs tables explaining why the data structures are designed that way, and Recommended Settings contrasted against package defaults before examining detailed simulations and diagnostics.
  3. Consult Measurement models and evidence for the mathematical derivations behind M-MOM standard-error propagation, latent connectivity measurement models, multi-lag calibration, autocorrelation estimation, and validation scope.

All code examples, figures, and diagnostic tables in this book are generated directly from the standalone medusa package (library(medusa)). See Reproduction and references for instructions on re-running the simulations and rendering the book from book/.
