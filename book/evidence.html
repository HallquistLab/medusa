Validation and the limits of the evidence

The standalone medusa package is evaluated through two complementary layers of evidence:

  1. Controlled mechanism demonstrations across Chapters 3–12 of this book, which isolate individual pipeline operators—cross-run HRF and 2-pass REML AR(\(p\)) learning, event-informed vs. blind deconvolution, adaptive rwls = "auto" spike downweighting, epoch target/collateral demixing, innovation-based spatial parcel pooling, mixed-effects vs. multi-lag trajectory calibration, and M-MOMS connectivity/PPI sensitivity—under known mathematical ground truth.
  2. The 14-study end-to-end MEDuSA Validation Suite, which benchmarks full pipeline configurations across realistic event schedules, neurovascular heterogeneity, heavy-tailed artifact bursts, censoring masks, and sample sizes.

Keeping the scope of each test distinct is essential: an algebraic check verifies that operator covariance propagation is exact conditional on a fitted noise model, whereas a Monte Carlo study measures bias and interval coverage under its specific data-generating process.

Evidence layer What it evaluates How to use it in practice
Studies 1–4 (Validation suite) Trial-slope recovery, precision weighting, sub-TR timing sensitivity, connectivity artifacts, and participant vs. trial sample-size scaling Compare estimator bias, RMSE, power, and false-positive calibration under realistic trial counts and ISI distributions.
Studies 5–9 (Validation suite) Realistic multi-run BOLD generation, HRF basis/regularization ablations, parcel spatial weighting, and full-pipeline configuration stress tests Guide sensitivity analyses when adapting medusa to a study’s specific TR, event density, and tSNR.
Study 10, Step 6, and Measurement-error models Predictor disattenuation (M-MOMS), shared_error = "auto" cross-parcel innovation subtraction, HKSJ participant pooling, and task-modulated shared-error PPI stress tests Verify disattenuation against naive slopes and run shared-error sensitivity curves before claiming latent PPI modulation.
Study 11 and Multi-lag trajectory calibration medusa_multilag_infer() available-case lag pooling, studentized Wishart Max-|T|, 1D TFCE, omnibus Mahalanobis tests, simultaneous bands, and "unsmoothed" window contrasts Distinguish single-trajectory familywise error (p_maxT, p_tfce) from study-wide multiplicity across parcels and covariates ("holm" / "BY").
Study 12 and Woolrich noise review 2-pass post-selection REML AR(\(p\)) noise refitting, Winsorized GLS marginal noise_sd, gapped Toeplitz covariance, and comparison against tapered residual estimators Audit empirical residual ACFs and spatial homogeneity when choosing spatial_groups and nuisance bases.
Studies 13–14 and Step 1 / Step 2 3D physiological HRF manifold (preset = "medusa") with positive-lobe normalize = "area" gain consistency, event-informed vs. blind deconvolution, and adaptive heavy-tail rwls = "auto" frame weighting Retain preset = "medusa" and rwls = "auto" as defaults so neurovascular dispersion, negative undershoot variations, and isolated motion spikes do not distort trial amplitudes.

Designing a Study-Specific Calibration Check

Before making high-stakes inferential claims on a novel task schedule—especially rapid event-related designs with variable-duration phases or trial-level PPI—run a compact synthetic calibration tailored to your exact study geometry:

  1. Lock the estimand and multiplicity family upfront: Prespecify the target parcels, peri-event lag grid, behavioral covariates, planned time windows, and study-wide adjustment rule ("holm" or "BY") before inspecting empirical \(p\)-values.
  2. Use your actual empirical event schedule and censor masks: Simulate BOLD on the exact empirical onset times, reaction-time durations, run lengths, and framewise-displacement censor masks (valid_rows) from your cohort so collinearity and edge truncation match your real data.
  3. Simulate both active signals and realistic structured nulls: Include voxelwise HRF variation across the physiological manifold, AR(\(p\)) temporal noise, spatially correlated parcel innovations, and—for Step 6 PPI—a stress null where shared physiological/motion noise varies across task conditions while true neural modulation is zero.
  4. Refit the complete pipeline inside every Monte Carlo replicate: Keep Step 1 HRF/noise learning, Step 2 lambda and rwls = "auto" estimation, Step 3/4 epoch demixing and parcel weighting, and Step 5/6 inference inside the loop rather than freezing oracle operators.
  5. Report bias, 95% CI coverage, null rejection, and convergence health together: Track missing edge-lag participants, isSingular() rates in mixed_by(), step-up regularization triggers in decon_connectivity_repair(), and binomial Monte Carlo intervals around empirical rejection and coverage rates.
