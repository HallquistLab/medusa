Appendix A — Reporting MEDuSA in Empirical Papers and Reviewer FAQ

Stage-by-stage reporting checklist, adaptable Methods templates, and answers to common peer-review questions

Overview

Clear methodological reporting serves two goals in empirical task-fMRI papers: it allows readers to reproduce every analytical choice from raw BIDS/fMRIPrep derivatives to group statistics, and it preempts common peer-review concerns about hemodynamic circularity, motion scrubbing, trial overlap, and measurement-error propagation.

This appendix provides three practical resources for manuscript preparation:

  1. A stage-by-stage reporting checklist (Table A.1) paired with a short R snippet to extract cohort-level quality-control numbers.
  2. Adaptable Methods prose templates covering Steps 0–4 preprocessing/deconvolution, Step 5 multilevel and trajectory inference, and Step 6 connectivity/PPI disattenuation.
  3. A Peer-Review FAQ addressing eight methodological questions frequently raised by cognitive neuroscience reviewers.

1. Stage-by-Stage Reporting Checklist

Extracting Cohort QC Descriptives in R

Before drafting your Methods section, summarize censoring, deconvolution gain, and trial retention across your analyzed cohort (\(s = 1, \dots, S\)):

# Assuming `subject_bundles` (Step 0), `subject_decons` (Step 2), and `subject_epochs` (Step 4) are named lists:
censor_pct <- unlist(lapply(subject_bundles, function(x) x$confounds$summary$pct_censored))
self_gain  <- unlist(lapply(names(subject_decons), function(id) {
  diagnose_crossrun_recovery(subject_decons[[id]], events = subject_bundles[[id]]$events)$summary$mean_gain
}))
gated_pct  <- sapply(subject_epochs, function(ep) 100 * mean(is.na(ep$panel$decon_demixed)))

cat(sprintf(
  "Censored volumes: M = %.1f%% (SD = %.1f%%)\nConditional self-gain: Mdn = %.2f (IQR = %.2f)\nGated trial-lags: M = %.1f%% (SD = %.1f%%)\n",
  mean(censor_pct), sd(censor_pct),
  median(self_gain, na.rm = TRUE), IQR(self_gain, na.rm = TRUE),
  mean(gated_pct), sd(gated_pct)
))

2. Adaptable Manuscript Methods Templates

Replace bracketed placeholders ([...]) with your study’s acquisition and design parameters, and omit optional clauses that do not apply to your analysis.

Template A: Steps 0–4 — Ingestion, Cross-Run HRF Learning, Deconvolution, and Parcel Readout

Single-trial fMRI deconvolution and parcel panel construction. Preprocessed BOLD time series (fMRIPrep [vXX.X], [MNI152NLin2009cAsym], TR = [0.80] s, [R = 4] runs per participant) were analyzed in R using the medusa package ([v0.1.0]; Hallquist, 2026). Within each run, [non-aggressive ICA-AROMA denoising was applied to BOLD volumes and matched CSF/WM nuisance regressors (Pruim et al., 2015; Lindquist et al., 2019), and] [24-parameter head-motion regressors plus cosine drift bases] were projected on an intact acquisition clock (read_fmriprep_subject()). [To prevent respiration-induced pseudomotion from triggering false framewise-displacement (FD) scrubs at sub-second TR, realignment parameters were notch-filtered across 12–20 breaths/min prior to computing FD (Fair et al., 2020).] Initial non-steady-state volumes and frames exceeding FD > [0.3 / 0.5] mm (M = [X.X]%, SD = [X.X]% of volumes) were masked via clock-preserving observation weights (valid_rows) rather than physically concatenating runs, and missed/timeout trials were retained as a separate nuisance event condition in the forward schedule.

Regional hemodynamic response functions (HRFs) were estimated out of sample across runs (estimate_crossrun_hrf()) over a three-dimensional physiological HRF library (hrf_library(preset = "medusa"), positive-lobe normalized) pooled within [Schaefer 200-parcel regions / bilateral cortical-ribbon neighborhoods]. Conditioned on each run’s out-of-sample selected HRF, temporal noise autocorrelation was refit in a second restricted-maximum-likelihood (REML) \(\text{AR}(p)\) pass (ar_order = "auto"). Continuous latent neural activity was reconstructed on the acquisition grid via prewhitened, Sobolev-penalized generalized least squares (deconvolve_crossrun_hrf(), scaling = "noise_sd"), using monotone SNR-stratified cross-run generalized cross-validation (lambda_mode = "auto") and task-protected outlier downweighting (rwls = "auto").

Event-locked trial estimates around [feedback onset] (lags = [-2 to +6 s]) were extracted via decon_epoch_regression() (source_model = ["impulse" / "duration"], readout_kernel = "triangle", restore_scale = TRUE), subtracting collateral spillover from non-target task phases (demix_other_events = TRUE) and inverting target-to-target hemodynamic mixing (demix_target_trials = TRUE, demix_ridge = [0.05]) to place trial readouts (decon_demixed) on an additive measurement-error scale (Vig & Hallquist, 2026). Voxels were aggregated into anatomical parcels using cross-run precision weights while propagating stationary temporal and spatial noise innovations into run-centered trial standard errors (se_demixed) and cross-parcel error correlations (unit_error_cor). Trial-lags with fewer than 20% unmasked causal HRF support (min_bold_coverage = 0.20; [X.X]% of trials) were recorded as missing (NA).

Template B: Step 5 — Population Multilevel and Peri-Event Trajectory Inference

Group-level trial and trajectory inference. Time-varying behavioral predictors ([reward prediction error, RPE]) were decomposed into within-person centered fluctuations ([rpe_within]) and person-level means ([rpe_mean]).

[Option 1 — Planned lag or duration mixed models (mixed_by()):] At each [parcel and planned lag], unshrunken trial readouts (decon_demixed) were fit using linear mixed-effects models (mixed_by() via lmerTest; Kuznetsova et al., 2017) weighted by normalized inverse total trial variance (weights = "precision_weight_cohort"), with random intercepts and within-person slopes per participant and nested run intercepts (decon_demixed ~ rpe_within + rpe_mean + (1 + rpe_within || id) + (1 | id:run)). Degrees of freedom were estimated via Satterthwaite’s approximation, and \(p\)-values across [P = 200 parcels] were controlled using [Holm familywise error / Benjamini–Yekutieli false discovery rate] adjustment.

[Option 2 — Joint multi-lag trajectory inference (medusa_multilag_infer()):] Full peri-event lag profiles ([-2 to +6 s]) were modeled jointly using medusa_multilag_infer() (trajectory_mode = "auto"), combining spectral cross-lag deblurring and uncertainty-propagated penalized splines with [799] finite-sample studentized Wishart draws across available participant lag sets. Within each trajectory, simultaneous 95% confidence bands (Max-|T|), 1D threshold-free cluster-enhanced (1D TFCE) \(p\)-values, and Mahalanobis epoch omnibus tests (omnibus_p) were computed; planned window contrasts ([late 3–5 s minus early 0–2 s]) were evaluated on unsmoothed lag estimates (contrast_source = "unsmoothed"). Across tested parcels, epoch-wide discovery was controlled by applying [Holm / Benjamini–Yekutieli] correction to omnibus_p.

Template C: Step 6 — Trial-Level Functional Connectivity and PPI

Measurement-error-corrected trial connectivity and PPI. Trial-to-trial seed–target coupling and psychophysiological interaction (Target ~ Seed + [Modulator] + Seed:[Modulator]) between [seed parcel] and [target parcel(s)] were estimated using decon_connectivity_repair() (method = "mmoms", shared_error = "auto", orthogonalize_seed_x = [FALSE / TRUE], use_whitened = FALSE). Within each participant, run-centered predictor measurement-error covariance blocks \(\Psi_i\) constructed from squared trial standard errors (se_demixed^2) were subtracted from sample design cross-products (\(X^\top X\)), while stationary cross-parcel error covariances estimated from task-free deconvolution innovations (unit_error_cor) were subtracted from predictor–outcome cross-products (\(X^\top Y\)) with step-up positive-definiteness regularization (Modified Method of Moments with Step-up Regularization, M-MOMS; Fuller, 1987; Vig & Hallquist, 2026). Participant-level M-MOMS slopes were pooled across the cohort (\(S = [N]\)) using leverage-guarded Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman (HKSJ) random-effects \(t\)-intervals, reported alongside uncorrected ordinary least-squares ("naive_unweighted") estimates and sensitivity analyses across hypothesized condition-dependent cross-error correlations.

3. Peer-Review FAQ: Common Questions and Misconceptions

Q1. Does evaluating peri-event lags every 0.5 s or 1.0 s mean MEDuSA achieves sub-TR neural temporal resolution?

No. Evaluating lags on a fine peri-event grid (or across jittered sub-TR event onsets) provides smooth phase alignment relative to stimulus onset, not unlimited neural frequency bandwidth. Because hemodynamic convolution \(H_r\) is a strong low-pass filter and Step 2 applies a positive smoothness penalty \(\lambda_v > 0\) to keep variance finite, recovered neural activity \(\widehat{a}_r(t)\) has a finite temporal point-spread width determined by the resolution operator \(R_r = D_r M_{r,o} H_r\). You can report that effective bandwidth transparently via diagnose_crossrun_recovery(decon) (mean_gain) and rely on medusa_multilag_infer(..., trajectory_mode = "auto") for regularized cross-lag deblurring (Step 2; Step 5).

Q2. Isn’t estimating the HRF from the participant’s own task scans circular (“double dipping”)?

No, for two independent reasons. First, estimate_crossrun_hrf() is strictly out of sample across runs: the HRF kernel applied to deconvolve run \(r\) is selected exclusively from non-target runs \(s \neq r\). Second, HRF selection evaluates overall task-evoked variance explained by event occurrences without access to downstream trial-by-trial behavioral modulators (rpe_within), group contrasts, or connectivity targets. Pass-2 \(\text{AR}(p)\) noise refitting on run \(r\) keeps the cross-fitted HRF completely frozen (Step 1; Autocorrelation Prewhitening).

Q3. How does MEDuSA differ from Mumford et al. (2012) LSS beta-series or a standard parametric GLM?

On slow, well-spaced, single-phase designs where the canonical HRF matches regional vasculature, parametric GLMs, LSS beta-series, and MEDuSA yield closely aligned group inferences. They diverge in three common cognitive neuroscience settings (Conceptual foundations §5): (1) rapid multi-phase trials (cue \(\to\) variable-RT decision \(\to\) feedback), where separate LSS regressions omit modulators on neighboring phases and absorb cue/RT confounds; (2) regions with non-canonical HRF peak delays or dispersion, which MEDuSA learns out of sample per parcel; and (3) biphasic or delayed post-event dynamics that collapse to near zero under a single canonical GLM impulse but emerge clearly across a continuous peri-event lag trajectory.

Q4. Empirical-Bayes shrunken decon_eb has lower trial-wise MSE—why shouldn’t I use it as the outcome in mixed_by()?

Minimum mean-squared error for individual trial predictions is not the same goal as unbiased structural slope estimation across trials (Skrondal & Laake, 2001; Vig & Hallquist, 2026). Because decon_eb shrinks noisy deviations toward the parcel mean by reliability factor \(\kappa_{ri} = \widehat{\tau}^2 / (\widehat{\tau}^2 + s_{ri}^2) < 1\), entering decon_eb on the left-hand side of a regression multiplies the true population slope by \(\bar{\kappa} < 1\)—attenuating group effects by 30–60% when single-trial fMRI reliability is moderate (QC Gallery Pair 3). Entering unshrunk decon_demixed with weights = "precision_weight_cohort" avoids shrinkage bias while still downweighting noisy trials and runs optimally.

Q5. Why set restore_scale = TRUE in Step 3 if Step 2 already standardized each run with scaling = "noise_sd"?

Standardizing each run by its marginal observation-noise SD \(\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{obs}}\) inside Step 2 places the data fidelity term and smoothness penalty \(\lambda_v \|P a_r\|^2\) on a dimensionless SNR scale so GCV smoothing behaves consistently across scanners and runs. If left in noise-SD units for Step 3–5, however, a noisy high-motion run (\(\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{obs}} = 2.0\)) would have its true neural signal divided by twice as much as a clean run (\(\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{obs}} = 1.0\)), shrinking its amplitude relative to clean runs. Setting restore_scale = TRUE multiplies both decon_demixed and se_demixed back by \(\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{obs}}\), placing every run of a parcel onto common physical BOLD units while letting se_demixed and precision_weight_cohort downweight noisy runs cleanly (Step 3; Propagating BOLD Noise into Trial SEs).

Q6. How does Step 6 M-MOMS PPI differ from standard gPPI (McLaren et al., 2012) or LSS beta-series correlations?

Standard BOLD-level PPI multiplies noisy BOLD (or MAP-deconvolved point estimates) by task regressors without accounting for two opposing trial-level error biases: (a) random estimation variance \(s_{S,i}^2\) in the seed readout attenuates both baseline connectivity and PPI interaction slopes toward zero, and (b) correlated physiological, vascular, or motion noise shared across brain regions (\(r_{ST} > 0\)) inflates apparent coupling. decon_connectivity_repair() forms the interaction on de-mixed trial readouts, subtracts analytical seed error moments \(\Psi_i(s_{S,i}^2)\) from \(X^\top X\), subtracts cross-parcel innovation covariance from \(X^\top Y\) (shared_error = "auto"), protects small-sample matrix conditioning (method = "mmoms"), and pools participant slopes via HKSJ \(t\)-intervals (Step 6; Latent Connectivity Beyond Per-Trial Disattenuation).

Q7. Can I interpret the first lag with p_tfce < .05 as the exact onset time of a neural effect?

No. Just as in cluster-based permutation testing for EEG/MEG and spatial fMRI (Sassenhagen & Draschkow, 2019), 1D threshold-free cluster enhancement (p_tfce) integrates contiguous extent and height across lags to boost sensitivity to sustained deflections; its error guarantee applies to the cluster-enhanced statistic rather than exact onset boundaries. When you want strict familywise localization at individual lags of a trajectory, inspect p_maxT and the simultaneous band [conf.low.sim, conf.high.sim]; when you want continuous timing summaries (peak lag, center-of-mass latency, or full-width at half-maximum), report $latency bootstrap intervals from medusa_multilag_infer() (Simultaneous Bands Across Lags).

Q8. Does Sobolev smoothing across motion-censored frames “hallucinate” trial observations inside scrubbed gaps?

No. Under observation_mask = "fit", censored frames (valid_rows == FALSE) contribute zero data weight during Step 2 while keeping acquisition timestamps exact. In Step 3, decon_epoch_regression() computes the exact fraction of each trial-lag’s forward HRF energy supported by unmasked BOLD frames: if fewer than 20% of causal HRF support was observed (min_bold_coverage = 0.20), that trial-lag is gated directly to NA; if partial scrubbing occurred inside the HRF window, the propagated sandwich variance \(\Omega_{r,\tau}\) automatically inflates se_demixed for that specific trial so downstream precision weights downweight it proportionately (Step 3; QC Gallery Pair 3).
